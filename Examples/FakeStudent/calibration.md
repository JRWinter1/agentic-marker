# Calibration Example — FakeStudent (Fabricated)

This is a fabricated example used for pre-calibration when no real calibration examples are available. It demonstrates the expected marking standards and feedback style.

## Question Summary

Students were asked to choose a real or simulated project they have been involved in (or have knowledge of) and produce a 2,500-word essay addressing four learning outcomes relating to project management: knowledge of PM components and theories (LO1), application of research and planning principles (LO2), understanding of project control stages including milestones, gates, and risk assessment (LO3), and critical reflection on project efficacy covering quality, cost, time, and risk (LO4).

## Overall Mark: 60%

## Marks Awarded

| Criterion | Mark |
|---|---|
| Knowledge & Understanding (40%) | 62% |
| Criticality (30%) | 58% |
| Reading and Research (20%) | 62% |
| Writing Style (10%) | 60% |

## Feedback Style

Below are two representative LO feedback excerpts showing the expected tone, specificity, and balance of praise and constructive criticism.

### LO1 — Knowledge & Understanding

**What we asked for:** Demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the essential components and theories associated with project management.

**How you did:** Very good knowledge of core project management concepts was demonstrated throughout. You drew appropriately on PMBOK, Burke, Lock, and Turner to support your discussion of scope management, stakeholder theory, and risk management. The identification of scope creep as the central issue in the restaurant conversion was well grounded in theory. However, the discussion would have been strengthened by engagement with PRINCE2 or the APM Body of Knowledge as structured PM methodologies, rather than relying primarily on individual academic texts. Formal PM tools such as Gantt charts and Critical Path Analysis were not discussed despite being core components of PM knowledge at this level.

### LO3 — Control Stage

**What we asked for:** Apply knowledge and understanding of the control stage of a project including milestones, gates, and incorporate how risk assessment models are critical to project planning and control.

**How you did:** The risk management discussion was the strongest element of this section, with appropriate use of Hillson and the PMBOK risk knowledge area. The probability-impact matrix was referenced but not applied to the project in a structured way. Milestones and stage gates were not addressed at all despite being explicitly required by the brief. PRINCE2's stage-gate model would have been directly relevant to the phased renovation but was not discussed. This is a significant omission that limited the mark for this outcome.

## Overall Summary Style

> Overall, this was a good submission that demonstrated very good knowledge of project management concepts applied to a credible hotel renovation context. The three-issue structure was well chosen and the referencing was appropriate. The main limitation was the uneven coverage of the brief's requirements: risk management was well handled but milestones and stage gates were absent from LO3, and the critical reflection in LO4 did not systematically address cost and quality alongside time and risk. Broadening the theoretical base to include professional body frameworks and formal PM tools would strengthen the work further.

## Feedforward Style

The feedforward was structured as a numbered list of actionable improvements:

1. Address all named elements in each learning outcome — milestones and stage gates were absent from LO3 despite being explicitly required.
2. Incorporate formal PM tools — a WBS was mentioned but a Gantt chart, CPA, or risk matrix applied to the project would demonstrate applied knowledge.
3. Engage with professional body frameworks — PRINCE2 and the APM BoK are expected at Level 5 alongside academic texts.
4. Reflect systematically on all four dimensions in LO4 — quality and cost need the same analytical depth as time and risk.

## Key Calibration Notes

- This submission sat in the **good (60–69%)** band — solid knowledge with room for deeper critical analysis.
- The primary strength was a **well-grounded, credible case study** with specific operational detail (costs, timelines, named stakeholders).
- The primary weakness was **incomplete coverage of the brief** — milestones and stage gates absent, quality and cost underexplored in reflection.
- Reading was appropriate but not wide-ranging — professional body frameworks (PRINCE2, APM BoK) were missing.
- The feedback tone is encouraging but clear about gaps — strengths acknowledged, then specific omissions identified.
- A 60% mark means the work is competent and shows understanding, but needs broader theoretical engagement and more systematic coverage to reach the 70s.
